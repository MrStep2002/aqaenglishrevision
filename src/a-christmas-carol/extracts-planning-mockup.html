Open the extract

Practise the decisions you’ll make.

  1. Read the question. Decide what you actually need to show.
  2. Choose your evidence. Look for words that give you something to say.
  3. Take it elsewhere. Connect the extract to a revealing moment in the wider novella.
  4. Build an argument. Turn the connections into a route through your answer.

Before the exam

Learn the decisions.

A quotation bank can help you remember. This workshop helps you decide what to do with what you remember.

Try the same extract with a second question. Keep a quotation if it still earns its place; change the argument you build around it.

About this practice

These are practice questions written for this workshop. The two-quotation exercise is a manageable rehearsal, not a required essay structure or an exam quotation quota. An outline still needs to become your own developed argument.

In the exam, respond to the question you are given and connect the extract with the wider text. The resources here support preparation for that independent response.
